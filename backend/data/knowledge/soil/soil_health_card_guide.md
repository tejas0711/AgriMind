# Understanding Soil Health Card Parameters

## Nitrogen (N)
Using the alkaline potassium permanganate method (the standard for Indian Soil Health Cards),
available nitrogen below 280 kg/ha is classified as LOW, 280-560 kg/ha as MEDIUM, and above
560 kg/ha as HIGH. Nitrogen is highly mobile in soil and easily lost through leaching and
volatilization, so it is usually recommended in split doses (basal + top-dressing) rather than a
single application.

## Phosphorus (P)
Using the Olsen method, available phosphorus below 10 kg/ha is generally LOW, 10-25 kg/ha
MEDIUM, and above 25 kg/ha HIGH. Phosphorus is largely immobile in soil, so it should be placed
close to the root zone at the time of sowing (basal application) rather than top-dressed later.

## Potassium (K)
Using the ammonium acetate (NH4OAc) method, available potassium below 108 kg/ha is generally
LOW, 108-280 kg/ha MEDIUM, and above 280 kg/ha HIGH. High potassium soils rarely need
additional potash fertilizer regardless of crop, and adding more can create nutrient imbalances
with calcium and magnesium uptake.

## Soil pH
Most field crops and vegetables perform best in a pH range of 6.0-7.5. Below 5.5 the soil is
considered acidic and may need lime application; above 8.0 it is alkaline and may need gypsum or
organic matter to improve structure and nutrient availability.

## Organic Carbon (OC)
Organic carbon below 0.5% is LOW, 0.5-0.75% MEDIUM, and above 0.75% HIGH. Low organic carbon
reduces the soil's water-holding capacity and nutrient buffering, and is best corrected over
multiple seasons through compost, FYM, or green manuring rather than a single application.

## Electrical Conductivity (EC)
EC below 1.0 dS/m is considered safe (non-saline) for most crops. Between 1.0-2.0 dS/m,
salt-sensitive crops (onion, most vegetables) may show reduced yield. Above 2.0 dS/m, only
salt-tolerant crops such as cotton or barley should generally be considered without remediation
(gypsum application, improved drainage, leaching irrigation).

## Micronutrients (Zinc, Iron, Sulphur)
Unlike N/P/K, micronutrients are typically reported against a single critical level rather than
three bands: below the critical level is DEFICIENT, at or above it is SUFFICIENT/ADEQUATE.
Commonly cited DTPA-extractable critical levels are Zinc 0.6 ppm, Iron 4.5 ppm, and Sulphur
10 ppm (CaCl2-extractable). Zinc deficiency is common in high-pH and heavily-cropped soils and is
usually corrected with zinc sulphate (basal or foliar spray); iron deficiency is more common in
calcareous/alkaline soils and often responds better to foliar application than soil application,
since soil-applied iron can become locked up again quickly at high pH.
