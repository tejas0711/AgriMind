# ICAR Guidelines on Soil Testing, Critical Limits, and Nutrient Deficiency Management

Source: Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) — Indian Institute of Soil Science (IISS), Bhopal. *Soil Testing in India: A Laboratory Manual and Fertilizer Management Guidelines.*

## National Fertility Rating Limits

Indian agricultural soils are classified into Low, Medium, and High fertility categories based on standard laboratory extraction methods:

| Parameter | Extraction Method | Low (Deficient) | Medium (Sufficient) | High (Excessive) | Unit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Available Nitrogen (N) | Alkaline Permanganate (Subbiah & Asija) | < 280 | 280 – 560 | > 560 | kg/ha |
| Available Phosphorus (P) | Olsen's 0.5M NaHCO3 (for pH > 6.5) | < 10 | 10 – 25 | > 25 | kg/ha |
| Available Potassium (K) | 1N Neutral Ammonium Acetate (NH4OAc) | < 108 | 108 – 280 | > 280 | kg/ha |
| Organic Carbon (OC) | Walkley-Black Wet Oxidation | < 0.50 | 0.50 – 0.75 | > 0.75 | % |
| Available Sulphur (S) | 0.15% CaCl2 Extractable | < 10.0 | 10.0 – 20.0 | > 20.0 | ppm (mg/kg) |

## Micronutrient Critical Limits (DTPA Extractable)

Micronutrients are evaluated against critical thresholds below which economic crop response to fertilization is consistently observed:

| Micronutrient | DTPA Critical Limit | Common Deficiency Symptoms | Recommended Corrective Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Zinc (Zn) | 0.60 ppm | Interveinal chlorosis, stunted terminal growth ("khaira" in rice, white bud in maize) | Basal zinc sulphate (ZnSO4 21%) @ 25 kg/ha or foliar spray 0.5% ZnSO4 with lime |
| Iron (Fe) | 4.50 ppm | Interveinal chlorosis on young upper leaves, bleaching under severe deficiency | Foliar spray of 1% FeSO4 (ferrous sulphate) + 0.1% citric acid; avoid soil application in high pH |
| Manganese (Mn) | 2.00 ppm | Grey speck or chlorotic spots between veins on younger leaves | Foliar spray of 0.5% MnSO4 (manganese sulphate) |
| Copper (Cu) | 0.20 ppm | Dieback of terminal twigs, chlorosis of leaf tips, poor grain filling | Basal copper sulphate @ 5–10 kg/ha or foliar spray 0.1% CuSO4 |
| Boron (B) | 0.50 ppm (Hot water) | Brittle leaves, cracking of stems, failure of fruit/pod setting | Basal borax @ 10 kg/ha or foliar spray of solubor @ 0.1–0.2% |

## Soil Reaction (pH) and Reclamation Guidelines

- **Acidic Soils (pH < 5.5):** Characterized by aluminium and manganese toxicity and phosphorus fixation. Agricultural lime (calcium carbonate) or dolomite is recommended based on exchangeable acidity. Over-liming must be avoided to prevent zinc and boron deficiencies.
- **Neutral Soils (pH 6.5 – 7.5):** Optimum for maximum availability of macronutrients (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S) and active microbial biodiversity.
- **Saline Soils (EC > 2.0 dS/m, pH < 8.5):** Require leaching of excess soluble salts with good quality irrigation water and provision of adequate field drainage. Chemical amendments are not required unless sodicity is also present.
- **Sodic/Alkaline Soils (pH > 8.2, ESP > 15):** High sodium content disperses soil clay, destroying structure and water infiltration. Gypsum (calcium sulphate) application is essential to displace exchangeable sodium with calcium, followed by flushing.

## Safe Agricultural Principles

1. Never apply chemical fertilizers containing nutrients that are already tested High in the soil report.
2. Never apply lime to soils that test neutral or alkaline (pH > 7.0).
3. Always integrate organic amendments (FYM, compost, green manure) with inorganic inputs to sustain biological soil health and micro-flora.
4. Fertilizer dosages must be treated as provisional guidelines; final quantities depend on target yield, crop variety, and local KVK verification.
